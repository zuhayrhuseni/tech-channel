# Asset sourcing rules for monetized videos

Operational rules for adding images, icons, footage, music, and SFX to episodes. Every claim is labeled:

- `[license-text]` — verified against the actual license page/document
- `[official]` — platform/agency documentation or policy page
- `[practitioner]` — reported experience from working creators (forums, community threads)
- `[folklore]` — widely repeated, plausible, but not verified against a primary source

Monetized YouTube = commercial use. Every asset must clear a *commercial* license, not just "free for personal use."

---

## 1. Source tiers

### Tier A — use freely, no attribution required

| Source | Terms | Gotchas |
|---|---|---|
| **Unsplash** (free) | Free commercial use, no attribution: "download, copy, modify, distribute, perform, and use images... for free, including for commercial purposes, without permission from or attributing the photographer." `[license-text]` https://unsplash.com/license | May not sell unmodified copies or compile images to replicate a competing service. `[license-text]` No model/property releases are collected, no indemnification (max liability ~$100 on free tier vs $10k/file on Unsplash+). `[practitioner]` https://picdefense.io/resources/source-intel/unsplash/, https://help.unsplash.com/en/articles/2612329-releases-and-trademarks |
| **Pexels** | Commercial use OK, "Attribution is not required." `[license-text]` https://www.pexels.com/license/ | Don't sell unaltered copies; don't show identifiable people "in a bad light"; "Don't imply endorsement of your product by people or brands on the imagery"; don't redistribute on other stock platforms; don't use as part of a trademark. `[license-text]` |
| **Pixabay** | Free, no attribution, modifications allowed. `[license-text]` https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/ | No standalone redistribution where "no creative effort has been applied"; content containing "recognisable trademarks, logos or brands" cannot be used commercially in relation to goods/services; no misleading/deceptive use. `[license-text]` |
| **NASA imagery** | "NASA content – images, audio, video... generally are not subject to copyright in the United States." Free for commercial editorial use. `[official]` https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/ | Must not "explicitly or implicitly convey NASA's endorsement"; NASA insignia may not appear "with AI generated imagery or in the training of AI tools"; identifiable astronauts/employees can't endorse products. `[official]` |
| **BLS (and most .gov data/charts)** | "Everything that it publishes... is in the public domain, except for previously copyrighted photographs and illustrations." Citation requested, not required. `[official]` https://www.bls.gov/opub/copyright-information.htm | BLS *photographs* are licensed Shutterstock/Adobe stock — public domain does NOT cover them. `[official]` |
| **YouTube Audio Library** (standard-license tracks) | "If you're in the YouTube Partner Program, you can monetize videos with music and sound effects from the Audio Library"; standard tracks "won't be claimed by a rights holder through the Content ID system." `[official]` https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882 | CC-licensed tracks inside the library DO require crediting the artist in the description. `[official]` |

**Rule A1.** Tier A images still carry third-party risk the license doesn't clear: people (publicity rights), logos/trademarks, and recognizable buildings inside the photo. For this channel: prefer Tier A photos with no faces and no visible brands. If a brand appears, use it only in editorial context about that brand (see §2). `[license-text]` (all three platforms warn about this) `[practitioner]` https://www.licenseorg.com/blog/free-stock-photos-licensing-traps

**Rule A2.** Never re-upload Tier A assets anywhere as a collection, and never build a "stock pack" from them — that's the one thing all three licenses prohibit. `[license-text]`

### Tier B — free but attribution is a license condition

| Source | Terms |
|---|---|
| **Flaticon (free tier)** | Attribution required "in the definitive project." For video: "designed by {Author} from Flaticon" next to the content if possible, otherwise "in the credits or acknowledgements section" — video description qualifies. TV/video use explicitly permitted. `[license-text]` https://www.flaticon.com/license/license.pdf (summarized via https://www.studocu.com/row/document/university-of-nairobi/introduction-to-computer-systems/license-sjdksd/41368136) |
| **Icons8 (free tier)** | Free commercial use *with a link to Icons8*; free accounts must provide the hyperlink(s) generated by the site — in a video's description for video use, and it "must be legible." Paid subscription removes the requirement. `[license-text]` https://intercom.help/icons8-7fb7577e8170/en/articles/5534926-universal-multimedia-license-agreement-for-icons8 |
| **Font Awesome Free** | Icons: CC BY 4.0. Fonts: SIL OFL 1.1. Code: MIT. "Downloaded Font Awesome Free files already contain embedded comments with sufficient attribution" — but in a rendered video the embedded comment is gone, so add a description credit. Brand icons: "do not use brand logos for any purpose except to represent the company, product, or service to which they refer." `[license-text]` https://github.com/FortAwesome/Font-Awesome/blob/6.x/LICENSE.txt |
| **The Noun Project (free)** | Free downloads are CC BY — credit the creator (credit line ships with the download) — or pay ($4.99/icon or Pro subscription) for a royalty-free, no-attribution license. `[official]` https://help.thenounproject.com/hc/en-us/articles/200509798 |
| **Wikimedia Commons** | License varies per file: PD, CC BY, CC BY-SA, GFDL. Check the file page every time. CC BY/BY-SA require attribution "in any reasonable manner"; if the uploader specified a credit format, use it. `[official]` https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia |

**Rule B1.** CC BY 4.0 attribution = TASL: **T**itle, **A**uthor, **S**ource link, **L**icense (+ note if modified), "not in any way that suggests the licensor endorses you." Put a `Credits` block in every video description; the annotate-script pipeline should emit one line per Tier B asset used. `[license-text]` https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

**Rule B2.** Avoid CC **BY-SA** and any **NC** (non-commercial) or **ND** (no-derivatives) asset entirely. NC is incompatible with a monetized channel; ND arguably prohibits compositing into a video; BY-SA's "modifications must also be similarly freely licensed" creates murky obligations for a rendered video. There is always a BY or PD alternative. `[license-text]` https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia

**Rule B3.** Missing attribution on Tier B assets is *copyright infringement*, not a formality — the license condition fails and you have no license. This is the single most common self-inflicted error with Flaticon/Noun Project assets. `[folklore]` (widely repeated; consistent with license mechanics)

### Tier C — never use

- Google Images / random web images, "no copyright intended" reuploads, fan-made asset packs. `[folklore]` (but see YouTube's own myth-busting: credit and disclaimers don't create a license — https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148 `[official]`)
- "Royalty-free" music from random YouTube channels: YouTube explicitly warns "YouTube is not responsible for issues that arise from 'royalty-free' music and sound effects from YouTube channels or other music libraries." `[official]` https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882
- Anything where the license can't be traced to a canonical page in ~2 minutes.

## 2. Logos and screenshots of real companies/products

This channel constantly references public companies. The legal frame:

- **Trademark** protects against consumer confusion about source/sponsorship. **Copyright** protects the creative artwork of a logo. Different regimes, different risks. `[official]` https://www.dmlp.org/legal-guide/using-trademarks-others
- **Nominative fair use** (trademark): you may use a mark to refer to the markholder if (1) the product isn't identifiable without it, (2) you use only what's necessary, (3) nothing suggests sponsorship or endorsement. Editorial commentary, criticism, and education are the core protected uses. `[official]` https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominative_use, https://www.dmlp.org/legal-guide/using-trademarks-others
- YouTube forwards trademark complaints to the uploader before acting and does not remove commentary/criticism uses that look like fair use. Trademark complaints against genuine editorial content are rare in practice. `[official]` https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6154218 `[practitioner]` https://www.upcounsel.com/youtube-trademark-infringement

**Rule L1.** Show a company's logo only when talking *about that company*, sized/used no more than needed, never implying they sponsor the channel. Never put a third-party logo in the channel banner, watermark, or as a thumbnail's dominant element implying partnership. `[official]`

**Rule L2.** Source logos from Wikimedia Commons and prefer files tagged `PD-textlogo` (simple text/geometric logos below the threshold of originality — no copyright, only trademark, which nominative use covers). These are clean SVGs. Files also carry a `{{trademarked}}` disclaimer — that's expected. `[official]` https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Template:PD-textlogo

**Rule L3.** Product/website screenshots in explainer context: transformative editorial use with commentary is the standard practice across tech YouTube (Fireship-style rapid UI captures with narration); fair use is fact-specific, not automatic, and Content ID/complaint systems "can't decide fair use." Keep captures short, annotated, and narrated — the commentary *is* the defense. `[official]` https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148 `[practitioner]` https://www.mangoapps.com/articles/screen-capture-copyright-violation-or-fair-use

**Rule L4.** (House rule interaction) Every screenshot/terminal capture must use a neutral home path and fake credentials per CLAUDE.md — this is an anonymity rule layered on top of the licensing rules.

## 3. Charts from public data

- Facts and data are not copyrightable (*Feist*): "since facts do not owe their origin to an act of authorship, they are not original and, thus, are not copyrightable." A chart's *specific visual design* can be. `[official]` https://askus.library.tulsacc.edu/faq/273107
- **Rule C1.** Always rebuild charts as Remotion components from raw data. Never screenshot someone else's chart (a bank's, a blog's, Statista's — Statista screenshots are a known claim magnet `[folklore]`).
- **Rule C2.** BLS: public domain, cite "Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics." `[official]` https://www.bls.gov/opub/copyright-information.htm
- **Rule C3.** FRED: check the series tag. `Public Domain: Citation Requested` → fine. `Copyrighted: Citation Required` or `Pre-approval Required` (third-party series like S&P, Moody's) → **do not use for commercial purposes without permission from the data owner**; the St. Louis Fed "cannot give you such permission." Prefer pulling the underlying series from the primary government source and charting it yourself. `[official]` https://fred.stlouisfed.org/legal, https://fredhelp.stlouisfed.org/fred/graphs/share-my-fred-graph/cite
- **Rule C4.** Company filings (10-K, S-1): the numbers are facts — chart them freely with a "Source: XYZ Corp 10-K, FY2025" cite. Don't reproduce the filing's own designed graphics. `[official]` (Feist principle applied) — and per CLAUDE.md rule 4, any derived claim that isn't directly in the filing gets a `[VERIFY]` tag in the script.

## 4. AI-generated imagery

- YouTube requires disclosure for *realistic* synthetic content: content that "makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do," "alters footage of a real event or place," or "generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur." `[official]` https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491
- Disclosure NOT required for: clearly unrealistic/animated content, beauty filters, color adjustment, and "production assistance, like using generative AI tools to create or improve a video outline, script, thumbnail, title, or infographic." `[official]` (same URL)
- Non-disclosure penalties: forced labels, content removal, or "suspension from the YouTube Partner Program." `[official]`
- Monetization (July 15, 2025 "inauthentic content" policy, renamed from "repetitious content"): AI-assisted videos remain monetizable; what's demonetized is mass-produced, repetitive, low-transformation content — the static-image + AI-voice compilation pattern. Human-authored script, narration, and editing keep a channel safe. `[official]` https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392 `[practitioner]` https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/

**Rule AI1.** AI-generated *illustrative* imagery (abstract diagrams, stylized scenes, obviously non-photoreal art) needs no disclosure label. Never generate photoreal depictions of real people, real companies' offices, or real events for this channel; if one is ever used, tick the altered-content disclosure at upload. `[official]`

**Rule AI2.** Don't composite NASA insignia with AI imagery (explicit NASA prohibition). `[official]` https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/

**Rule AI3.** Check generator terms: some image tools restrict commercial use on free tiers. Verify the specific tool's license before first use. `[folklore]` (varies per tool; verify per-tool)

## 5. Music and SFX

- **YouTube Audio Library** is the only music source YouTube itself guarantees Content ID-safe. Standard tracks: no attribution, monetization OK. CC tracks in the library: credit in description. `[official]` https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882
- **Subscription libraries (Epidemic Sound, Artlist):** content published *during* an active subscription stays cleared forever; content published *after* cancellation using the same tracks gets claimed. Epidemic requires channel safelisting while subscribed — an unsafelisted channel gets claims even with a valid license (cleared by dispute, but it lands first). `[official]`/`[practitioner]` https://www.epidemicsound.com/how-it-works/license-music-legally/, https://thepostflow.com/post-production/video-editing/music-subscriptions-compared/
- **CC music (e.g., Kevin MacLeod/Incompetech):** legal to use with CC BY attribution, but third parties have fraudulently registered his unregistered tracks in Content ID and claimed videos; visible attribution + a saved license copy is the dispute evidence. `[practitioner]` https://incompetech.com/music/royalty-free/faq.html, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_MacLeod
- Even *paid* stock audio gets claimed: Adobe Stock audio flagged on YouTube by Content ID partners, with ~30-day dispute cycles. `[practitioner]` https://community.adobe.com/t5/stock-discussions/adobe-stock-audio-assets-are-being-flagged-for-copyright-claims-on-youtube/m-p/14743816
- Content ID claims ≠ strikes: a claim can block, monetize (revenue diverted to claimant), or track — it doesn't strike the channel; strikes come from formal takedown notices. `[official]` https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370

**Rule M1.** Default: YouTube Audio Library standard tracks for music and SFX. If a subscription library is adopted later: safelist the channel immediately, keep license PDFs per episode, and never publish with its tracks after the subscription lapses.

**Rule M2.** For any non-Audio-Library track, archive proof (license page snapshot, receipt, download date) in the episode directory *before* rendering. Claims are won with documentation. `[practitioner]`

## 6. Screen recordings of software/websites

- UI chrome of an OS/app is copyrighted expression in theory; in practice, narrated editorial screencasts (tutorials, reviews, commentary — the Fireship format) are ubiquitous and treated as transformative fair use; claims against them are effectively unheard of for software UI. `[practitioner]`/`[folklore]` https://www.mangoapps.com/articles/screen-capture-copyright-violation-or-fair-use
- Real risk in screencasts is *embedded third-party media*: a site autoplaying music or video during your capture triggers Content ID matches on that media, not the UI. `[folklore]` (consistent with how Content ID matches audio/video fingerprints `[official]`)
- Some sites' TOS prohibit scraping/recording; a TOS breach is a contract issue with that site, not a copyright strike vector on YouTube. Streaming services with DRM (Netflix etc.) are the exception — never capture DRM'd content. `[folklore]`

**Rule S1.** Screencast freely for editorial content; mute system audio during capture; never capture DRM streams; keep captures narrated and purposeful. Neutral paths and fake credentials always (CLAUDE.md rules 1–3).

## 7. Practical workflow for this pipeline

- **Icons beat one-off images.** A single coherent icon set (Font Awesome Free + one paid or attributed set) covers most explainer needs with consistent stroke/style; hunting individual PNGs produces style drift and per-asset license tracking overhead. `[practitioner]` https://thepostflow.com/post-production/video-editing/music-subscriptions-compared/ (same principle across asset types)
- **SVG for icons/logos/illustration, high-res raster for photos**: "ship SVG for every logo, icon, and illustration, and ship optimized PNG for every photo." SVGs scale losslessly under Remotion zoom/pan; for 1080p renders, raster photos should be ≥1920px wide — 2560px+ if the component animates scale, so zoom never exceeds source resolution. `[practitioner]` https://vectosolve.com/blog/png-vs-svg-when-to-use-each `[folklore]` (2x-headroom rule is standard practice)
- **Asset manifest per episode.** Keep `episodes/NNN-slug/assets/` with a `credits.md` listing: filename, source URL, license, attribution string (if Tier B), retrieval date. The description credit block is generated from it. This mirrors how professional channels survive disputes: the evidence exists before the claim does. `[practitioner]`

### Pre-flight checklist (run per episode)

1. Every asset traceable to Tier A/B source or self-made? (No Tier C.)
2. Tier B attribution lines in `credits.md` → description block?
3. No CC NC/ND/BY-SA assets?
4. Photos: no identifiable faces/brands used out of editorial context?
5. Logos: editorial reference only, PD-textlogo SVG where possible, no implied endorsement?
6. Charts rebuilt from raw data with source cite; no third-party FRED series?
7. Music/SFX from Audio Library or documented license archived in episode dir?
8. AI imagery non-photoreal, or disclosure ticked at upload?
9. Screencasts: system audio muted, no DRM content, neutral paths, fake credentials?
10. Any unverifiable factual claim in visuals marked `[VERIFY]`?
